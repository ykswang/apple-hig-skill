# Inclusion

> Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion · Inclusive apps and games put people first by prioritizing respectful communication and presenting content and functionality in ways everyone can access and understand.

## When to use / core idea
- Apply when reviewing any copy, imagery, characters, forms, and experiences; inclusion is iterative — examine your assumptions and stay open to evolving understanding.
- Use empathy to see how people with different perspectives respond; a word or image may be incomprehensible or carry unintended meaning.
- Perspectives arise from shared characteristics: age; gender and gender identity; race and ethnicity; sexuality; physical attributes; cognitive attributes; permanent, temporary, and situational disabilities; language and culture; religion; education; political or philosophical opinions; social and economic context.
- Don't frame the work as merely a search for offensive content — an inoffensive app isn't necessarily inclusive. Aim for a welcoming experience.
- Companion pages: accessibility (disability support), writing (tone), right-to-left and localization.

## Rules

### Welcoming language
- **Consider the tone of your copy from different perspectives** — style communicates almost as much as words; e.g., an academic tone can suggest only highly educated people are welcome. Be clear, direct, respectful.
- **Pay attention to how you refer to people** — use *you*/*your* to address people directly; *the user*/*the player* feels distant. Consider reserving *we*/*our* for your software or company; otherwise they can suggest a personal relationship that may read as insulting or condescending.
- **Avoid using specialized or technical terms without defining them** — if you must use them, define them first and make definitions easy to look up; plain language is easier to read and translate.
- **Replace colloquial expressions with plain language** — they're culture-specific, hard to translate, and some have exclusionary origins (e.g., *peanut gallery*, *grandfathered in*); even benign ones exclude those who don't understand them.
- **Consider carefully before including humor** — subjective and hard to translate; risks confusing, irritating (on repetition), or insulting people.

### Being approachable
- Don't require particular skills or knowledge up front; give a clear path to deepen understanding over time.
- Present a clear, straightforward interface that fits each platform (see designing-for-* pages).
- Build in ways to learn: consider an onboarding flow that lets newcomers go step by step while letting others skip straight to content.

### Gender identity
- Avoid unnecessary references to specific genders. E.g., replace "You can let a subscriber post his or her recipes…" with "Subscribers can post recipes…" — gender-neutral nouns and no singular gendered pronouns also localize better into gendered languages.
- Avoid a specific gender in avatars, emoji, glyphs, or game characters where possible; prefer giving people tools to customize them.
- To depict a generic person, use a nongendered human image (generic person = human, not man or woman); SF Symbols offers nongendered person/figure glyphs.
- Most apps don't need gender. If required (e.g., health or legal reasons), consider inclusive options such as *nonbinary*, *self-identify*, *decline to state*, and let people specify pronouns.

### People and settings
- Portray a range of human characteristics and activities (e.g., fitness moves demonstrated by people of different racial backgrounds, body types, ages, physical capabilities).
- Avoid stereotypical depictions of occupations or behaviors (e.g., only male doctors, female nurses, or heroes/villains perpetuating racial or gender stereotypes).
- Review settings and objects: high affluence can feel unwelcoming/out of touch; when it makes sense, prefer places, homes, activities, and items familiar and relatable to most people.

### Avoiding stereotypes
- Become aware of your biases so you recognize where they influence design decisions.
- Don't assume narrow definitions (e.g., *family* = a woman, a man, and their biological children).
- Avoid context-specific experiences not everyone shares (e.g., security questions like "favorite subject in college", "make of your first car", "how did you feel when you first saw a rainbow"); prefer universal ones ("favorite activity", "name of your first friend", "quality that describes you best").

### Accessibility
- Support Apple's accessibility features — VoiceOver, Display Accommodations, closed captioning, Switch Control, Speak Screen.
- Never assume a disability prevents someone from wanting to use your experience.
- Each disability is a spectrum (e.g., visual: low vision to complete blindness, color blindness, blurry vision, light sensitivity, peripheral vision loss).
- Everyone can experience disabilities: age-related, *temporary* (short-term hearing loss from infection), *situational* (can't hear on a noisy train).
- **Avoid images and language that exclude people with disabilities** — include people with disabilities when representing variety; avoid language that uses a disability to express a negative quality.
- **Take a people-first approach when writing about people with disabilities** — describe accomplishments and goals before mentioning a disability; find out how a specific person or community self-identifies.
- **Prioritize simplicity and perceivability** — prefer familiar, consistent interactions; ensure content is perceivable via sight, hearing, or touch.

### Languages
- Internationalize (handle other languages and regions, including date, time, money formatting) and provide translated text and resources per locale.
- Inclusive practices (plain language, no unnecessary gender references, diverse representation, avoiding stereotypes and culture-specific content) ease localization.
- Use SF Symbols glyphs to streamline localization — many language-specific glyphs plus LTR/RTL variants.
- Colors carry culture-specific meaning (e.g., white = death/grief in some places, purity/peace in others); if color communicates, ensure it communicates the same thing in each localized version.

## Platform considerations
No additional considerations: iOS, iPadOS, macOS, tvOS, visionOS, watchOS.
