# Designing for macOS

> Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-macos · People rely on the power, spaciousness, and flexibility of a Mac for in-depth productivity, media, and games, often using several apps at once.

## Device characteristics
- **Display** — typically large and high-resolution; workspace extendable with additional displays, including iPad.
- **Ergonomics** — used while stationary, usually on a desk/table; viewing distance about 1 to 3 feet.
- **Inputs** — any combination of physical keyboards, pointing devices, game controls, and Siri.
- **App interactions** — from a few minutes of quick tasks to several hours of deep concentration. Multiple apps open; people expect smooth transitions between active and inactive states when switching apps.
- **System features** — the menu bar, file management, going full screen, Dock menus.

## Best practices
- **Leverage large displays** to present more content in fewer nested levels and with less modality, while keeping a comfortable information density that doesn't strain viewing.
- **Let people resize, hide, show, and move windows**; support full-screen mode for a distraction-free context.
- **Use the menu bar** to give easy access to all the commands in your app.
- **Support high-precision input** for pixel-perfect selections and edits.
- **Handle keyboard shortcuts** to accelerate actions and enable keyboard-only work styles.
- **Support personalization** — customizable toolbars, window configurations showing the most-used views, and choice of interface colors and fonts.
