# Design principles

> Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles · Fundamental principles that guide design across Apple platforms and underpin all other HIG guidance.

## When to use / core idea
- Principles are tools for weighing competing priorities and making key decisions — there's no one right way to apply them.
- Eight principles: **Purpose** (Make something meaningful), **Agency** (Let people do things their own way), **Responsibility** (Act in people's best interest), **Familiarity** (Build on what people know), **Flexibility** (Adapt to diverse contexts and needs), **Simplicity** (Be clear and direct), **Craft** (Care about every detail), **Delight** (Make it human).

## Rules

### Purpose — Make something meaningful
Identify what matters most to the people you design for and make those things great.
- **Create value** — at every stage, ask what the product is for and whether the design serves that purpose.
- **Keep focused** — prioritize the most important features, aligned with how people want to use the app, and make them truly great; a product with a clear use is more effective.
- **Find new ways to solve the problem** — investigate existing solutions and avoid re-creating them; define what sets your product apart and reflect it in the design.

### Agency — Let people do things their own way
Give freedom to act, keep people informed, make recovery easy.
- **Stay out of the way** — get people directly to the task or content; the best designs are unobtrusive and present when needed.
- **Give people the freedom to explore** — don't lock people into specific flows or modes; when a guided flow is necessary, make it easy to skip or escape.
- **Help people recover from mistakes** — let people reverse actions / return to a previous state; build in forgiveness; recovering shouldn't cost time or work.

### Responsibility — Act in people's best interest
Earn trust by prioritizing safety and privacy and being transparent.
- **Be fully transparent about what your product does and why** — make intentions clear from the first interaction; give a clear rationale when asking for permission; be clear about what data you collect and how you use it.
- **Keep people's information safe** — collect only what the product needs to function; handle it with care; anticipate misuse/harm and put protections in place.

### Familiarity — Build on what people know
Ground the experience in established physical and digital patterns, applied consistently.
- **Use concepts that people know** — draw on real-world and other-software knowledge.
- **Keep visuals and interactions consistent** — once an element's behavior/appearance is established, apply it throughout.
- **Provide clear feedback** — show when controls are available, indicate when content changes, and use system patterns for alerts and choices.

### Flexibility — Adapt to diverse contexts and needs
Be mindful of experiences other than yours; support as many devices, interaction types, and perspectives as possible.
- **Design for everyone** — account for diverse experiences, perspectives, and needs; treat accessibility as a priority from the start; design inclusively.
- **Preserve a person's context** — across platforms and configurations, keep content and controls in consistent, predictable positions; use natural animations to ease transitions.
- **Consider a variety of input methods** — voice, touch, keyboard, and more.
- **Approach every platform with intention** — give each supported platform the same level of care so it feels polished and at home.

### Simplicity — Be clear and direct
Remove the unnecessary; every element earns its place; logical organization and straightforward navigation.
- **Include just what's necessary** — simplicity isn't minimalism; keep important things close and let the others fall away.
- **Be concise** — choose exactly the words needed to convey a concept or label a control.
- **Establish hierarchy** — make form and function apparent; prioritize recognizable controls and consistent structure so people know where they are and what comes next.

### Craft — Care about every detail
- **Quality sets the tone** — be deliberate; strive for stunning visuals, smooth animations, precise wording, thoughtful audio.
- **Experiment and iterate** — prototype early, try new approaches, discard what doesn't work; test in real-world settings for durability, reliability, performance.
- **Maintain your craft** — shipping isn't the finish line; keep current with the latest platform capabilities and design patterns.

### Delight — Make it human
People remember how a product makes them feel.
- **Identify the emotion you want to inspire** — e.g., fitness energizes, meditation calms, a game thrills; let it shape the design.
- **Create defining moments** — from a button press to an error message, consider adding a touch of character that reflects the design's spirit.
- **Don't mistake delight for decoration** — don't let delight for its own sake get in the way of the core purpose; balance practical touches vs. whimsy by experimenting.
- **Consider the whole** — delight is the sum of freedom to act, safety to explore, familiar metaphors, and flexibility across contexts.
