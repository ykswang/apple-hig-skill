# Designing for visionOS

> Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-visionos · With Apple Vision Pro, people engage with apps and games in an infinite 3D space while staying connected to their surroundings.

## Device characteristics
- **Space** — limitless canvas for windows, volumes, and 3D objects; people can enter deeply immersive experiences.
- **Immersion** — apps launch by default in the *Shared Space* (multiple apps side by side; people open, close, relocate windows). People can move an app to a *Full Space* (only app running), where they can view 3D content blended with surroundings, open a portal to another place, or enter a different world.
- **Passthrough** — live video from external cameras; people control the amount with the Digital Crown.
- **Spatial Audio** — system models the room's acoustics so audio sounds natural; with permission to access surroundings info, apps can fine-tune Spatial Audio.
- **Eyes and hands** — most actions: look at an object with the eyes + *indirect* gesture (e.g., tap). Also *direct* gestures (touching with a finger).
- **Ergonomics** — people see everything via cameras, so visual comfort is paramount. System places content relative to the wearer's head regardless of height or sitting/standing/lying down; visionOS brings content to people so they can remain at rest.
- **Accessibility** — VoiceOver, Switch Control, Dwell Control, Guided Access, Head Pointer, and more; system components include accessibility by default.
- **Safety (Important)** — consider the device's unique characteristics and user safety (see Apple Vision Pro User Guide). Not for use while operating a vehicle or heavy machinery, or while moving around unsafe environments (balconies, streets, stairs, other hazards). Designed for individuals 13 years of age or older.

## Best practices
- **Embrace the unique features of Apple Vision Pro** — use space, Spatial Audio, and immersion; integrate passthrough and eye/hand spatial input so it feels at home.
- **Consider different types of immersion for your most distinctive moments** — windowed/UI-centric, fully immersive, or in between. For each key moment find the *minimum* level of immersion that suits it; don't assume every moment needs full immersion.
- **Use windows for contained, UI-centric experiences** — prefer standard windows (planes in space with familiar controls) for standard tasks; people can relocate windows anywhere, and dynamic scaling keeps content legible near or far.
- **Prioritize comfort:**
  - Display content within the field of view, positioned relative to the head; avoid placing content where people must turn their head or change position to interact.
  - Avoid motion that's overwhelming, jarring, too fast, or missing a stationary frame of reference.
  - Support indirect gestures so people can interact with hands resting in the lap or at their sides.
  - If supporting direct gestures, keep interactive content not too far away and don't require interaction for extended periods.
  - Avoid encouraging people to move too much in a fully immersive experience.
- **Help people share activities with others** — with SharePlay, people see participants' *spatial Personas*, as if together in the same space.
