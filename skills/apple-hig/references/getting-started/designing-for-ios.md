# Designing for iOS

> Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ios · People depend on iPhone to stay connected, play games, view media, accomplish tasks, and track personal data anywhere and on the go.

## Device characteristics
- **Display** — medium-size, high-resolution.
- **Ergonomics** — held in one or both hands; people switch between landscape and portrait as needed; viewing distance no more than a foot or two.
- **Inputs** — Multi-Touch gestures, virtual keyboards, voice (Siri); people also expect use of personal data (with permission), gyroscope/accelerometer, and spatial interactions.
- **App interactions** — ranges from a minute or two (checking updates, tracking data, messaging) to an hour+ (browsing, games, media). Multiple apps open; people switch among them frequently.
- **System features** — widgets, Home Screen quick actions, Spotlight, Shortcuts, activity views.

## Best practices
- **Limit onscreen controls** so people concentrate on primary tasks and content; make secondary details and actions discoverable with minimal interaction.
- **Adapt seamlessly to appearance changes** — device orientation, Dark Mode, Dynamic Type — letting people choose the configurations that work best for them.
- **Support the way people hold the device** — controls in the middle or bottom of the display are easier to reach; so it's especially important to let people swipe to navigate back or initiate actions in a list row.
- **With permission, integrate platform capabilities instead of asking people to enter data** — e.g., accept payments, biometric authentication, location-based features.
